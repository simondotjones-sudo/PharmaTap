# Pharmacy Standard Operating Procedures: Ireland

*Prepared 5 October 2026. Not legal advice. Check the final list against current PSI guidance.*

## How the requirement works

No law or PSI document sets a closed list of SOPs. The requirement is that:

- The **superintendent pharmacist** ensures SOPs are in place and approves them
- SOPs are **specific to the pharmacy** and accurately describe how each task is done there
- Content is accurate against legislation, current PSI guidance and good pharmacy practice
- Staff are **trained on the SOPs** relevant to their role, with records
- SOPs are **version controlled**, reviewed, and readily available to staff, including locums

The PSI names a small core set (sourcing, storage, controlled drugs, disposal) and states its list is not exhaustive. The list below is the set an inspector would expect to find, split into universal and service-dependent.

Inspectors care less about the number of SOPs than about fit. A generic SOP that does not match actual practice is treated as a failing.

## 1. Medicines management

- Sourcing of medicines and supplier verification
- Receipt and storage, including fridge and cold chain monitoring
- Date checking and stock rotation
- Controlled drugs: ordering, receipt, storage, register, supply and destruction
- Disposal of medicines and patient returns
- Recalls, quality defects and Falsified Medicines Directive (FMD) alerts

## 2. Dispensing

- Prescription receipt and legal/clinical validation
- Assembly and labelling
- Final accuracy check
- Supply and patient counselling, including collection by a representative
- Owings and part-supplies
- Emergency supply
- High-risk medicines
- Record keeping (prescription, controlled drugs and duty registers)
- Dispensing errors and near misses

## 3. Non-prescription medicines

- Sale and supply, including referral to the pharmacist
- Codeine-containing products
- Other restricted products (for example pseudoephedrine, emergency contraception)

## 4. Governance

- Pharmacist absence and supervision
- Opening, closing, security and key holding
- Confidentiality and data protection
- Complaints handling
- Adverse reaction reporting to the HPRA
- Staff training and SOP sign-off, including locum induction
- Cleaning and hygiene
- SOP control: drafting, approval, versioning, review and withdrawal

## 5. Only if the service is offered

- Vaccination
- Emergency medicines
- Delivery of medicines
- Monitored dosage systems
- Supply to nursing homes and residential care
- Opioid substitution
- Extemporaneous dispensing
- Point of care testing, sharps and needle-stick
- Online supply
- Prescription extension
- Veterinary medicines

## Items to verify before relying on this list

- The PSI's current advice on SOPs and any newer service-specific guidance
- Whether the pharmacy offers services not listed in section 5
- Related documents that sit alongside SOPs but are not SOPs themselves, such as the Safety Statement and the Child Safeguarding Statement
